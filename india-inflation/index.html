64 Years of Indian Purchasing Power Destruction

The Purchasing Power Staircase -- Value of Rs 100 (1960)
TL;DR

Rs 100 today buys only what Rs 1.11 bought in 1960.

Prices have multiplied 90x in 64 years. But that's the average. Gold inflated 47x. Telecom deflated to zero. The rupee is not a single story -- it's many, wildly divergent ones.

Executive Summary

Prices multiplied 90x since 1960.

CPI went from 2.53 to 227.60 (2010=100). Average annual inflation: 7.3%. Every generation sees their parents' prices become unrecognizable.

Inflation is radically non-uniform.

Gold inflated 47x. Healthcare 10-25x. Cars only ~3x. Telecom saw infinite deflation. The "average" inflation number is a statistical fiction that describes nobody's actual experience.

Food prices are politically explosive.

Onions have toppled governments. Tomatoes hit Rs 250/kg in 2023. India's CPI basket is 46% food -- making food inflation the lived reality of 800 million people.

1974 was the worst year ever.

Inflation hit 28.6% -- the all-time peak. OPEC embargo + domestic drought + money supply expansion created a perfect storm. A salaried worker lost a quarter of their purchasing power in 12 months.

The rupee lost 81% against the dollar.

From Rs 17.50/USD in 1990 to ~Rs 94/USD in 2026. India's inflation has been consistently higher than US inflation, and the exchange rate tells that story relentlessly.

Inequality makes inflation regressive.

The bottom 50% spend 55-60% of income on food. The top 10% spend 25%. When food inflates 10%, it's a rounding error for the rich and a catastrophe for the poor. India's income Gini: 0.61.

Two consecutive monsoon failures, the Indo-Pakistan War of 1965, and a 36.5% rupee devaluation in June 1966. Food prices spiked as agricultural output fell 20%. PL-480 food aid from the US became a political humiliation. Resolution came with good monsoons and the arrival of Green Revolution seeds.

The First Oil Shock

1973 -- 1975

OPEC oil embargo, concurrent domestic drought, and expansionary monetary policy under Indira Gandhi. Real wages fell sharply. Essential commodities disappeared from markets. This economic distress was a major factor in Indira Gandhi declaring Emergency in 1975. Resolution came through Emergency-era price controls and better harvests.

Iranian Revolution, second oil price spike, growing fiscal deficits, and the drought of 1979. India's current account deficit widened. An IMF loan of SDR 5 billion was taken in 1981 -- the largest IMF loan at the time. Resolution came through gradual fiscal correction and falling global oil prices.

The Onion Theory of Indian Democracy

Onion prices have directly caused election losses. The 1998 Delhi election was partly decided by onions at Rs 60-80/kg. In 2019, Rs 150/kg onions triggered export bans. No other country has a vegetable with this much political power.

In July 2023, tomato prices hit Rs 200-250/kg in major cities. The government sold tomatoes through mobile vans at subsidized rates. Restaurants removed tomato from their menus. A single weather event demonstrated how fragile India's food price stability is.

Private education fee inflation runs at 10-12%/year -- roughly double the headline CPI (the official CPI education sub-index shows ~3-5%). A middle-class family that could comfortably afford IIM fees in the 1990s now faces Rs 27.5 lakh for the same program.

India's premier institutions were once nearly free. The Nehruvian philosophy treated education as a public good (whether that was sustainable is a separate debate). Over the decades, the subsidy model has been quietly dismantled, fee by fee, and the cost transferred to families. Add coaching classes (Kota coaching went from Rs 5,000 to Rs 2 lakh/year) and the total cost of aspiration has exploded.

The Jio Revolution of 2016 was India's single greatest deflationary event in any category, ever. Data costs fell 95% in 3 years, putting the internet in the hands of 500 million people who couldn't afford it before.

Historical Turning Points

June 6, 1966
The Day the Rupee Was Cut

The Indian rupee was devalued from Rs 4.76/USD to Rs 7.50/USD -- a 36.5% overnight cut. PM Indira Gandhi, under pressure from the World Bank and IMF who demanded devaluation as a condition for aid, made the deeply unpopular decision.

Import prices jumped instantly. The devaluation was meant to boost exports but instead triggered a political firestorm. It shaped India's distrust of the IMF for decades and became a defining moment in the country's post-independence economic psyche.

October 17, 1973
The Oil Weapon

OPEC announced its oil embargo. India, already struggling with drought, saw oil import bills explode. By 1974, inflation hit 28.6% -- the worst in independent India's history.

The economic distress contributed directly to the political unrest that Indira Gandhi would cite as justification for declaring Emergency in June 1975. Inflation doesn't just erode money -- it erodes democracy.

July 1, 1991
The Gold Airlift

With foreign exchange reserves at $1.2 billion -- barely enough for three weeks of imports -- the Reserve Bank of India airlifted 47 tonnes of gold to the Bank of England as collateral for a $405 million emergency loan. Another 20 tonnes went to the Union Bank of Switzerland.

It was India's most humiliating economic moment since independence. Within weeks, Finance Minister Manmohan Singh presented a budget that would dismantle the License Raj and open India to the world. The crisis gave birth to modern India.

November 8, 2016
Demonetization

At 8 PM, PM Narendra Modi announced that all Rs 500 and Rs 1,000 notes -- 86% of currency in circulation -- were no longer legal tender. The stated goal: eliminate black money and counterfeit currency.

The immediate effect was a massive demand contraction. CPI fell to 3.63% in the following months. Cash-dependent sectors were devastated. GDP growth dropped from 8.3% to 6.8%. The long-term inflation impact was temporary, but the economic disruption was enormous.

September 5, 2016
Jio Changes Everything

Mukesh Ambani launched Reliance Jio with free 4G data and calls. Within 6 months, 100 million subscribers. Within 2 years, India went from the world's most expensive mobile data market to the cheapest. Data prices fell 95%.

Three telecom operators merged or shut down. 500 million Indians who couldn't afford internet gained access. It was creative destruction at a scale unprecedented in any emerging market -- the only time in Indian history that a utility went from expensive to free.

Methodology Notes

  • CPI used is combined (rural + urban). Before 2011, CPI-IW used as proxy.
  • "Multiplier" calculations compare approximate prices at two points. They are illustrative, not precise.
  • Category-specific rates derived from sub-indices where available, observed market prices otherwise.
  • All rupee amounts in nominal terms unless stated as "real."

FAQ

Why CPI and not WPI?

CPI measures what consumers actually pay. WPI doesn't capture retail prices, services, or housing. RBI adopted CPI as the inflation target in 2016.

Is 7.3% average inflation high?

Yes. The US averaged ~3.5%. At 7.3%, prices double every 10 years. Over 64 years, that compounds to 90x vs ~9x.

Did liberalization help?

Eventually, yes. The 2000s and 2010s saw lower inflation than the 1970s-1990s. Competition, imports, and RBI's inflation targeting (2016) all contributed.
